## About me

I am a postdoctoral scholar in the Department of Management Science and Engineering at Stanford University and a Stanford Causal Science Center Fellow, working with [Vasilis Syrgkanis](https://vsyrgkanis.com/) and supported by Netflix Research. I completed my Ph.D. in Statistics at the University of Washington in June 2026, advised by [Marco Carone](http://faculty.washington.edu/mcarone/about.html) and [Alex Luedtke](http://www.alexluedtke.com), during which I was supported by a Netflix Graduate Research Fellowship. I also collaborate with [Nathan Kallus](https://nathankallus.com/) and [Aurélien Bibaut](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=N_8WC5oAAAAJ) at Netflix.

My research focuses on **causal inference and semiparametric statistics**, particularly how to use machine learning for valid and efficient statistical inference. I have developed methods for [automatic debiasing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11868) of smooth functionals of nonparametric M-estimands, efficient estimation and calibration of [heterogeneous treatment effects](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01972), calibrated debiased machine learning for [doubly robust inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02771), and adaptive inference after [data-driven model selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12544). My work also addresses a broad range of causal inference problems, including long-term causal inference through [instrumental variables with surrogate outcomes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07729) and [dynamic modeling in reinforcement learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06926), as well as data fusion to improve efficiency in randomized trials.

I also work on **dynamic decision-making and reinforcement learning**, with a focus on learning theory and statistical inference. In off-policy evaluation and offline reinforcement learning, I study the estimation, calibration, and model selection of occupancy ratios and value functions under function approximation and distribution shift, with an emphasis on avoiding restrictive [Bellman completeness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05375) assumptions. I also study debiased estimation and efficient inference in off-policy evaluation, inverse reinforcement learning, and dynamic discrete choice models.

**Calibration** is a recurring theme across my research. I study how to improve existing machine-learning estimates through post-hoc calibration, often without retraining the underlying models. My work has shown how calibration can improve [predictions of heterogeneous treatment effects](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/van-der-laan23a.html) and long-term policy values in offline reinforcement learning; improve [inverse probability weighting estimators](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v275/laan25a.html); increase statistical efficiency in prediction-powered inference and randomized trials; and weaken nuisance-estimation requirements for doubly robust inference. I have also developed calibration-based methods for conformal prediction and distribution-free uncertainty quantification.
